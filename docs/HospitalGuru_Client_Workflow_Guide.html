What to emphasize

Stock is controlled

Available quantities account for reserved, quarantined and expired stock. An invoice reserves stock; verified payment completes the sale and posts the inventory movement.

What to emphasize

Approvals have owners

Receiving, transfers, returns and payment verification pass to authorized roles. The requester cannot approve certain actions they initiated.

What to emphasize

Each location sees its work

Staff are assigned to role and location workspaces. Dashboards and lists use platform records and authorized access.

What to emphasize

History is traceable

Supported transactions create references and audit records, so teams can follow what happened and who handled the step.

Suggested opening

“HospitalGuru organizes pharmacy operations from replenishment through dispensing, with clear approval and payment handoffs and stock balances that reflect completed activity.”

02 / Procure & receive

From supplier order
to approved stock.

Use this flow to demonstrate how purchasing and central-store receiving stay separate, reviewable steps.

Purchase orderProcurement Officer
›
Approval & issueHead Pharmacist / Procurement
›
Goods received noteDRF Store Keeper
›
Approve & postHead Pharmacist
  1. Create a purchase order. Procurement selects the supplier and destination, adds medicines and quantities, and saves the order for authorized review.
  2. Approve and issue the order. The Head Pharmacist approves a pending order. Procurement then issues the approved order to the supplier.
  3. Record the delivery at the central store. The DRF Store Keeper starts a GRN and records supplier, invoice/delivery references, receipt date, medicine, batch, expiry, received/accepted/rejected quantities, and unit cost.
  4. Save the GRN draft, then submit it. A saved draft does not change inventory. Submit the completed GRN for approval.
  5. Review and post. The Head Pharmacist approves the receipt and posts it. Posting adds accepted quantities to central-store inventory and records the stock movement.
A

Check the delivery against the order

Match the supplier, purchase order, product, batch and quantity. Record rejected quantities and a reason where applicable.

B

Post only after review

Do not treat a draft or pending GRN as available stock. The approval and posting steps complete the receiving workflow.

Client demo narration

“The supplier delivery is captured first, but stock only becomes available after the receiving record is reviewed and posted. This preserves a clear receiving trail.”

Demo tip: use a sample supplier, purchase order and one medicine batch. Show the GRN status before and after approval/posting.

03 / Replenish pharmacy

Request, approve,
dispatch, receive.

The pharmacy asks for stock; the central store prepares and dispatches it; the destination confirms what physically arrived.

Request stockPharmacy Dispenser
›
Prepare transferDRF Store Keeper
›
ApproveHead Pharmacist
›
DispatchDRF Store Keeper
›
Confirm receiptPharmacy Supervisor
  1. Submit a pharmacy stock request. Select catalogue medicines, enter quantities, set routine/urgent/critical priority, and explain the request. Submission does not move stock.
  2. Prepare a transfer from the request. The DRF Store Keeper selects eligible central-store batches and quantities. The linked request limits which medicines and quantities can be issued.
  3. Submit for approval. A submitted transfer waits for authorized approval. Stock is not yet dispatched.
  4. Approve and dispatch. The Head Pharmacist approves; the DRF Store Keeper dispatches. Stock then moves into transit and is not yet available for sale at the destination.
  5. Confirm physical receipt. The Pharmacy Supervisor records the quantity received. Any short receipt needs a discrepancy note; the transfer can remain partially received until resolved.
If stock needs to go back

Use the return workflow

The pharmacy submits a reason, source batch, quantity and condition. The Head Pharmacist reviews it; the DRF Store Keeper records physical receipt; an authorized inspection sets the final disposition.

Possible disposition

Stock, quarantine or dispose

Sealed/good and unexpired items may return to usable stock. Other items can be quarantined or disposed according to the inspected condition.

Client demo narration

“A request is not a stock movement. The movement happens through approved dispatch, and the receiving pharmacy confirms the actual quantity at its end.”

04 / Dispense & bill

A controlled point
of sale workflow.

Demonstrate an outpatient sale first, then show how inpatient patient and prescription details can be linked when needed.

Select sale typeOutpatient / Inpatient
›
Select patient / RxFor inpatient sales
›
Add eligible stockBatch & quantity checks
›
Generate invoiceQuantity reserved
›
Submit & verify paymentCashier completes sale
  1. Choose the sale type. For inpatient dispensing, find and verify the patient record. Select an active prescription if one applies.
  2. Build the sale from eligible batches. Search by medicine or batch, review available quantity and price, then add the required quantity. Expired, quarantined and already reserved stock is excluded.
  3. Respect prescription balances. When linked, only medicines on that prescription can be added and quantities cannot exceed the remaining prescribed balance. Medicines marked as prescription-required need a valid linked prescription.
  4. Generate the invoice. The invoice reserves stock while payment is pending. If it is cancelled, the reserved quantities are released.
  5. Submit payment evidence. The dispenser records cash received or a bank/POS reference and submits it for verification.
  6. Verify independently. A cashier checks the payment and verifies or rejects it. The person who submitted the payment cannot verify their own submission.
  7. Complete and issue the receipt. Successful verification completes the sale, posts the inventory deduction and updates the linked prescription. Open the sale receipt for printing.
Show on screen

Live available stock

Batch expiry, available quantity, current selling price and invoice total are visible as the sale is built.

Explain the handoff

Payment is not self-approved

Evidence submission and cashier verification are separate actions, supporting a clear payment control.

Client demo narration

“The invoice reserves the selected batch, but the stock deduction is finalized only after an authorized cashier verifies the payment.”

05 / Reconcile & review

Close the day
with a clear record.

Use the supervisor and cashier workspaces to reconcile payment methods, resolve differences and preserve the close snapshot.

1

Open the business day

The Pharmacy Supervisor opens the controlled shift before the team records sales for the day.

2

Clear payment checks

The Cashier reviews pending evidence and records verification outcomes. Rejected attempts remain visible for follow-up.

3

Reconcile each tender

Compare expected totals for cash, bank transfer and POS terminal with the confirmed amounts. Explain any discrepancy.

4

Review inventory activity

Check recorded movement types such as sales, transfers, returns and adjustments against the day’s work.

5

Submit reconciliation

Save the cashier reconciliation for supervisor review. Outstanding payment checks must be cleared before the day can be closed.

6

Close the day

The Pharmacy Supervisor records notes and closes the day. The platform saves an audited close snapshot.

Oversight and accountability

  • Dashboards summarize authorized operational records.
  • Audit views show recent events, users, actions, references and outcomes.
  • Open system-health events and backup issues are surfaced to authorized administrators.
  • Administrative notifications appear in the account’s task list.
  • Role and location permissions shape which workspaces, records and actions are available.
Client demo narration

“The close brings payment and inventory activity together, while the audit trail lets authorized reviewers trace important actions back to their records.”

Before go-live

  • Confirm locations, user accounts, role permissions and approval owners.
  • Load and validate the medicine catalogue, suppliers, opening stock and reorder settings.
  • Agree cash, transfer, POS and day-close procedures with pharmacy and finance.
  • Train approvers and test a complete purchase-to-dispense scenario.
  • Configure verified email delivery before offering self-service password recovery.

Present scope honestly

  • Barcode scanning is not available in the current POS screen.
  • Password reset emails require a verified hospital email delivery service; until configured, staff should contact hospital IT.
  • Dashboard details depend on the corresponding platform records and the signed-in user’s permissions.
  • Agree clinical, financial and regulatory procedures with the hospital before production use.
